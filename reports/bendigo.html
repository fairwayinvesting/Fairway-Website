Bendigo sits at the foothills of the Great Dividing Range, 150km northwest of Melbourne. The V/Line Bendigo line connects the city directly to Southern Cross Station in approximately two hours — a commute that, while long for daily travel, positions Bendigo as a lifestyle and affordability alternative for Melbourne workers on hybrid arrangements. Bendigo is a fully self-contained regional city with its own hospital, university, arts precinct and growing professional employment base.

Greater Bendigo is a diversifying regional economy, anchored historically in manufacturing and mining but increasingly driven by healthcare, education and professional services. La Trobe University and Bendigo Health are the city's two largest employers, supporting a stable professional workforce. Population growth reflects both organic city growth and lifestyle migration from Melbourne.

The city's ongoing investment in arts, culture and public space — including the Bendigo Art Gallery and the Ulumbarra Theatre — has contributed to a genuine shift in liveability credentials, attracting a professional demographic that supports sustained rental demand.

Market conditions

Supply & demand snapshot

Bendigo operates primarily as a private sale market — auction volumes are low and clearance rate data is largely unavailable. The more relevant signals are days on market, vacancy, and months of supply, all of which point to a seller-favoured environment with limited available stock.

Rental demand is underpinned by Bendigo Health and La Trobe University employment — both institutional employers with long-term footprints that insulate the city from cyclical downturns in rental demand.

Comparable market precedent

Bendigo's profile — established regional city, diversified employment, significant healthcare and education infrastructure — shares characteristics with markets such as Ballarat and Launceston at comparable stages of their investment cycles. Past performance in those markets is not a guide to future performance in Bendigo.

Relative affordability

At ~$669k, Bendigo houses trade at a significant discount to both Melbourne and coastal lifestyle markets, with a yield profile that reflects genuine income generation. The city's self-contained economy means price growth is driven by local fundamentals rather than capital city proximity arbitrage.

What makes this market compelling?

What to watch

Risks & conditions to monitor

Economic concentration risk

Healthcare and education are the dominant employers. A reduction in Federal or State funding to these sectors could soften local employment and rental demand, though both institutions have long-term infrastructure commitments that provide significant forward visibility.

Melbourne commute viability

At approximately 2 hours, the Bendigo–Melbourne commute is at the outer limit for daily travel. Any shift toward mandatory return-to-office could reduce lifestyle migrant demand, though local tenant demand is largely employment-driven rather than commuter-driven.

Income-constrained growth ceiling

Bendigo's price growth is partly constrained by local income levels. Unlike markets with direct Melbourne pricing arbitrage, growth here is driven by local wage growth and serviceability — a more durable but less explosive dynamic than coastal or commuter belt markets.

Fairway's view

Luke's take

Luke Clifford

Founder, Fairway Investing

Bendigo is a city that often gets overlooked because it doesn't have a single dramatic catalyst like a fast rail announcement or a defence base. But what it does have is something arguably more durable: a diversified, stable employment base, a fully functional urban environment, and genuine income-generation potential for investors with a long hold horizon.

The selectivity here is around property type and location within the city. The inner ring, where properties sit close to healthcare and education employment with genuinely constrained land supply and a stable tenant base, is where the medium-term hold case is strongest. Flora Hill's proximity to La Trobe is an example of the kind of structural rental demand that holds through rate cycles.
