Service hub for western NSW

Dubbo sits at the confluence of the Macquarie River and the Newell and Mitchell highways, 397km northwest of Sydney. It is the dominant service hub for western NSW — a vast catchment of approximately 300,000 people across surrounding LGAs who travel to Dubbo for healthcare, retail, professional services and government. Dubbo Airport operates daily flights to Sydney, making the city more connected to the capital than many regional centres of comparable size. Wellington, 80km southeast via Mitchell Hwy, is a satellite township within Dubbo Regional Council — a distinct and affordable market within the same LGA.

Market conditions

Supply & demand snapshot

Dubbo operates as a private sale market — clearance rate data is not meaningful as auction volumes are negligible. The more telling signals are days on market (20 days for houses — very fast), vacancy rate (1.38%) and months of supply (1.5 months) — all pointing to a market firmly favouring sellers.

Rental demand is underpinned by healthcare employment, government services and an expanding energy sector workforce. The city's role as a logistics and service hub creates a diverse tenant base with limited exposure to any single sector downturn.

Comparable market precedent

Dubbo's profile — dominant regional hub, diversified employment, healthcare and government anchors, airport connectivity — shares characteristics with markets such as Orange and Tamworth at comparable stages of their investment cycles. Past performance in those markets is not a guide to future performance in Dubbo.

Relative affordability

Wellington at ~$450k represents one of the most affordable high-confidence entry points across all Fairway markets — with a 5%+ gross yield and 14.9% five-year CAGR. Dubbo city proper at ~$800k fits a more typical established regional city profile with strong rental demand and consistent 8.5% ten-year growth.

Investment case

What makes this market compelling?

Dubbo Regional Council offers two distinct investment propositions within a single LGA. Dubbo city proper is a liquid, established market with 1,366 house sales per year, consistent rent growth and a diversified tenant base anchored by healthcare and government employment. Wellington presents a high-yield, low-entry alternative — sub-$500k with 5%+ gross yield and growth that rivals markets trading at twice the price.

What to watch

Risks & conditions to monitor

Thin unit market liquidity

With only ~67 unit sales per year across the LGA, the unit market carries real liquidity risk. Fewer comparables makes valuations less reliable, and finding a buyer quickly when exiting is harder than in the house market. Houses are the preferred asset class in Dubbo for most investors.

Distance from Sydney — no rail connection

At 397km with no passenger rail, Dubbo cannot draw the commuter premium seen in regional cities with metro connectivity. Growth is driven by local fundamentals — healthcare, agriculture and government services — which is more stable but less leveraged to capital city spillover demand.

Agricultural sector cyclicality

Drought conditions in western NSW reduce agricultural employment and can dampen local spending, particularly in smaller townships like Wellington. Healthcare and government employment provide a significant buffer for Dubbo city, but Wellington investors should factor in this seasonal and cyclical exposure.

Fairway's view

Luke's take

Luke Clifford

Founder, Fairway Investing

Dubbo sits at an unusual intersection for a regional city — airport connectivity, a hospital that draws patients from a 300,000-person catchment, and two very different investment propositions within the same LGA. Dubbo city proper is a solid, liquid market with consistent tenant demand. Wellington is the one that genuinely surprised me — sub-$500k entry, 5%+ gross yield, and 14.9% five-year CAGR. Growth numbers like that at that price point are rare.

The key consideration with Wellington is that it's a small town of around 5,000 people, so property selection matters more than in a larger market. It's primarily a cash flow play — but the data shows you don't necessarily have to give up growth to get it. For investors who can be patient about exit timing and are selective about property quality, the entry price alone makes it worth a serious look.
