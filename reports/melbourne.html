This report covers two distinct but complementary Melbourne investment zones. The outer ring — Wyndham City suburbs including Wyndham Vale, Tarneit, Werribee and Point Cook — is one of Australia's fastest-growing population corridors, 25–35km southwest of the CBD. The inner west — Footscray, Yarraville, Sunshine, Coburg, Preston and Reservoir — sits 5–15km from the CBD with established infrastructure and high rental yields on sub-$650k unit entry.

The outer ring is a growth story driven by land supply, population and infrastructure. The inner west is a yield story, with the Airport Rail Link at Sunshine representing a structural infrastructure change yet to fully price in.

WYNDHAM CITY POPULATION TREND

Source: ABS Regional Population, 2021 Census & projections

Wyndham City is the fastest-growing local government area in Victoria, adding more than 10,000 new residents annually. The LGA's population has more than doubled since 2010, driven by greenfield land releases, relative affordability versus established suburbs, and strong demand from young families and new Australians.

The inner west suburbs (Footscray, Yarraville, Coburg, Preston, Reservoir) draw a different demographic — renters, young professionals and downsizers attracted by Melbourne address accessibility at sub-$650k unit entry. Inner west population growth is driven by densification rather than greenfield expansion.

Market conditions

Supply & demand snapshot

Wyndham City's clearance rate of 46% (May 2026) sits below the Melbourne-wide average, reflecting a mix of auction and private sale markets in the outer ring. Days on market average 42 days for houses — moderate — and 65 days for units, which is elevated and points to softer unit demand in this LGA. The more telling signal is the vacancy rate trend: falling from 4.49% in December 2025 to 2.85% by June 2026, a meaningful tightening of the rental market over just six months.

The inner west unit market (Footscray, Yarraville, Coburg, Preston, Reservoir) operates with higher auction volumes and stronger buyer competition than the outer ring, though clearance rates in 2026 reflect the broader Melbourne softness. Rental demand in inner west suburbs remains robust, underpinned by proximity to the CBD, university campuses and healthcare employment clusters.

Investment case

What makes this market compelling?

Melbourne's western corridor delivered the largest 1-year house price gains across all Fairway tracked markets — Wyndham Vale +17.0%, Tarneit +15.7%, Werribee +13.0%. Wyndham City's ~4.5% annual population growth is the underlying engine: more than 10,000 new residents per year, undersupplied land, and major infrastructure investment converging in a single LGA. The vacancy rate trend reinforces the picture — falling from 4.49% in December 2025 to 2.85% by June 2026, a rental market tightening faster than the headline figure implies.

The inner west unit market presents a different data profile: 5-year price growth has been subdued, but gross yields of 4.8–5.7% at sub-$650k entry are high by Melbourne standards. The Airport Rail Link at Sunshine is a structural infrastructure change that is yet to fully price into the surrounding suburbs.

What to watch

Risks & conditions to monitor

Outer ring unit market illiquidity

Wyndham City's unit market records only 639 annual sales at the LGA level, and units average 65 days on market versus 42 for houses. If the house strategy is viable, it is structurally preferred in this LGA. Inner west suburbs (Footscray, Yarraville, Preston) have materially higher unit liquidity.

Interest rate sensitivity in the outer ring

Wyndham City's owner-occupier buyer base is predominantly young families with high loan-to-value ratios. This cohort is more sensitive to interest rate movements than established suburb buyers. Rate increases disproportionately affect demand in growth corridor LGAs, which can create more pronounced price softness in a rising rate environment.

Inner west unit price growth track record

Five-year price growth across most inner west unit suburbs has been flat to negative. Footscray units -0.6% p.a. over 5 years, Yarraville -0.1% p.a. The yield is real; the capital growth case over the medium term is less supported by the current data set. The Airport Rail infrastructure case is structural but has a long timeline.

Outer ring new supply pipeline

Greenfield land supply in Wyndham is ongoing, which moderates the price premium that scarcity alone would generate. As new estates are released, entry-level properties in existing estates can face softness until the new releases are absorbed. Property selection within the LGA matters — established suburbs with limited new land outperform raw greenfield.

Fairway's view

Luke's take

Luke Clifford

Founder, Fairway Investing

The western growth corridor delivered the largest 1-year house price numbers across all our tracked markets — Wyndham Vale at +17%, Tarneit at +15.7%. These aren't one-off anomalies. Wyndham City adds more than 10,000 people a year to a land-constrained supply pipeline, and the West Gate Tunnel is materially changing the commute proposition. The vacancy trend is also worth noting: 4.5% in December, 2.85% by June. That's a rental market tightening faster than the number itself implies.

The inner west unit data is honest about the 5-year track record — flat to slightly negative price growth in most suburbs. But the yields are genuinely strong at 5–5.7% gross on sub-$600k Melbourne assets, and the Airport Rail at Sunshine is a structural change. Whether that is enough for a capital growth case depends on the investor's horizon. The data as it stands makes the yield argument more concrete than the growth argument for this part of the city.
