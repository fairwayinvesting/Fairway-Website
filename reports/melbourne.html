Melbourne’s inner suburbs form one of the strongest unit investment zones in Australia — 19 suburbs tracked across Inner West, Inner North, Inner East and Inner South, all within 5–20km of the CBD. Each zone is ranked by house-to-unit price disparity: the metric that shows where unit buyers get the most location per dollar.

Gross yields of 4.4–7.6% across the zone sit well above Melbourne’s inner-ring average, with the west and north offering the strongest income profile and the east offering the highest disparity ratios — Hawthorn at 3.41× is the standout of all 19 suburbs. The detailed inner west analysis and infrastructure section below covers that zone in depth.

Rental demand

Why renters choose the inner west

The inner west draws a broad, stable tenant base: healthcare workers from Footscray and Sunshine hospitals, students at Victoria University, CBD workers priced out of inner east and south, and young professionals attracted to established suburb infrastructure at sub-$700/wk rent.

This diversity of tenant demand insulates the zone from sector-specific downturns better than markets dependent on a single driver. The three-year compound rent growth of +6.5–9.7% p.a. across these suburbs reflects structural undersupply of quality rental stock relative to population growth in inner Melbourne.

Melbourne’s net overseas migration, running above 100,000 per year through 2024–26, continues to concentrate in established inner suburbs with direct public transport access — a profile that exactly describes Footscray, Coburg and Reservoir.

The Airport Rail case for Sunshine

Sunshine Station will be the primary interchange for the Airport Rail Link — the only stop between the CBD and the airport. Properties within walking distance of Sunshine Station represent a structural infrastructure play. The timeline is long (est. 2030s) but the project is approved and funded. Current unit prices at $542k median don’t yet reflect this outcome; the +20.1% one-year price move is an early market signal, though on thin volume (80 sales/yr).

Investment case

What makes this market compelling?

The inner-ring Melbourne unit case is a yield story first — but the profile varies by zone. Inner west and inner north deliver the strongest raw yields: Flemington at 7.6% gross, Thornbury at 5.9%, Coburg at 5.7%. Entry prices across these zones range from ~$435k to ~$660k, with most 2-bed units well below the all-bedroom medians shown in the tables. Inner east adds the disparity angle — Hawthorn’s 3.41× house-to-unit ratio is the highest of all 19 suburbs tracked, giving unit buyers rare access to a prestige inner-east address.

Inner south rounds out the picture. Malvern East (2.90× disparity) and South Yarra (1,162 unit sales/yr — the deepest liquidity pool of any suburb in the report) serve a higher entry-price client profile, but with strong prestige fundamentals and tenant quality. Across all four zones the consistent message is the same: income-first investors with realistic expectations and a long enough horizon are well-positioned.

What to watch

Risks & conditions to monitor

Subdued 5-year price growth track record

Five-year price CAGR is flat to negative in Footscray (-0.6%) and Yarraville (-0.3%), and modest in most other suburbs. The yield is genuine and well-supported; the capital growth case over a 5-year medium term is not. Investors should go in with income-first expectations.

Sunshine volume risk — treat the 1yr figure carefully

Sunshine’s +20.1% one-year price growth is based on only 80 annual unit sales — the thinnest market of the six. Thin markets amplify volatility in both directions. The Airport Rail thesis is structural; the current 1yr price move may reflect early positioning, but it shouldn’t be extrapolated as a trend.

New apartment supply pipeline

Melbourne has significant ongoing apartment construction. Inner west suburbs with large development sites could see increased rental supply, which may moderate rent growth in specific pockets. Property selection within each suburb matters — established stock in established streets tends to outperform new-build high-rise in both rent and resale.

Airport Rail timeline uncertainty

The Airport Rail Link is approved but estimated to deliver in the 2030s. Infrastructure timelines in Australia frequently extend. Sunshine’s investment case is not dependent on the rail delivering on schedule, but anyone pricing in near-term uplift should note this risk explicitly.

Fairway’s view

Luke’s take

Luke Clifford

Founder, Fairway Investing

When I sit down with a client and go through this market, the first thing I do is show them the disparity data — because it makes the whole case in about thirty seconds. Hawthorn: $2.4M house median, $704k unit median. Same suburb, same street, same school zone. That’s 29 cents in the dollar. Malvern East, Thornbury, Box Hill — different numbers, same story. You’re not buying a compromise; you’re buying the same postcode at a fraction of the price.

Where I’d direct someone within that depends on what they’re after. If yield matters most, I’d start in the inner west and north — Flemington at 7.6% is the standout in this report, and Coburg and Thornbury aren’t far behind at entry prices that still make sense. If the brief is a blue-chip address they can hold for twenty years, inner east and south — Hawthorn, Malvern East, South Yarra — is the answer, just at a meaningfully higher entry point. Either way, I’m having the same conversation: buy it for the income, hold it long enough for the location to do its job, and tune out the short-term noise.
