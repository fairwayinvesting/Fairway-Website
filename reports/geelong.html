POPULATION GROWTH TREND

Source: ABS Regional Population, 2021 Census & projections

Greater Geelong is one of Australia's fastest-growing regional cities, adding approximately 5,600 residents per year. The growth is driven by Melbourne affordability pressure, strong local employment anchors — including Deakin University (50,000+ students), Barwon Health, and a rapidly diversifying manufacturing base — and lifestyle migration from interstate.

ABS projections place Greater Geelong's population at 310,000 by 2031, representing sustained demand pressure on housing stock well into the next decade.

Comparable market precedent

Geelong's current profile — affordable entry relative to capital city, major infrastructure committed, strong population growth, tight vacancy — shares characteristics with markets such as Ipswich QLD and Newcastle NSW at comparable stages of their growth cycle. Past performance in those markets is not a guide to future performance in Geelong.

Relative affordability

At $916k, Greater Geelong now trades at near-parity with Melbourne's metro median — a convergence that reflects exactly what the infrastructure and population thesis predicted. For a self-contained regional city with direct rail access, a diverse employment base, and genuine population momentum 75km from the CBD, price alignment with Melbourne signals the market has re-rated Geelong on its own merits.

Investment case

What makes this market compelling?

Geelong is a dual-purpose market — it delivers genuine rental income today while carrying a strong medium-term growth case driven by infrastructure, population, and affordability dynamics that are difficult to find in the same market at this price point.

What to watch

Risks & conditions to monitor

Fast Rail timeline uncertainty

The Geelong Fast Rail remains a State Government commitment without a confirmed completion date. Political changes could delay or alter scope. The growth case holds without it, but the Fast Rail is a significant upside catalyst — not a guarantee.

Interest rate sensitivity

Like most regional markets, Geelong buyers are more rate-sensitive than inner-city markets. A sustained rate environment above 5.5% could soften demand from owner-occupiers, slowing price growth in the short term.

Armstrong Creek supply

The Armstrong Creek growth corridor is adding significant new housing stock. This can suppress rental yields and price growth in the outer-ring new-build segments. Established inner-ring properties are largely insulated from this supply pressure.

Fairway's view

Luke's take

Luke Clifford

Founder, Fairway Investing

When clients ask me about Geelong, I describe it as a city that doesn't need a single big catalyst to justify the investment case — it already has everything. Healthcare, education, advanced manufacturing, defence. The population growth comes from Melbourne overspill and genuine lifestyle migration, and the catalysts in the pipeline are funded and underway, not hypothetical.

Where I focus is the established inner and mid-ring, not the growth corridors. The outer suburbs have real supply coming through and developers are still active — that compresses both rental yields and resale. The tighter established pockets, where land is constrained and tenants tend to be professionals and healthcare workers, are where I think the hold case is strongest over the medium term regardless of what happens with interest rates.
