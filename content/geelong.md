## location

Geelong sits at the western edge of Port Phillip Bay, connected to Melbourne by the Princes Freeway and a frequent V/Line rail service. The Geelong Fast Rail project — when completed — will cut that journey to under 30 minutes, fundamentally repositioning this market relative to Melbourne's employment base.

---

## populationNarrative

Greater Geelong is one of Australia's fastest-growing regional cities, adding approximately 5,600 residents per year. The growth is driven by Melbourne affordability pressure, strong local employment anchors — including Deakin University (50,000+ students), Barwon Health, and a rapidly diversifying manufacturing base — and lifestyle migration from interstate.

ABS projections place Greater Geelong's population at 310,000 by 2031, representing sustained demand pressure on housing stock well into the next decade.

## investmentCase.body

Geelong is a dual-purpose market — it delivers genuine rental income today while carrying a strong medium-term growth case driven by infrastructure, population, and affordability dynamics that are difficult to find in the same market at this price point.

---

## investmentCase.comparableMarkets

Geelong's current profile — affordable entry relative to capital city, major infrastructure committed, strong population growth, tight vacancy — shares characteristics with markets such as Ipswich QLD and Newcastle NSW at comparable stages of their growth cycle. Past performance in those markets is not a guide to future performance in Geelong.

---

## investmentCase.relativeAffordability

At ~$760k median, Greater Geelong offers a meaningful affordability discount to Melbourne's metro median — for a city 75km away with direct rail access and a genuine independent economic base. Historical data shows this gap narrows as infrastructure improves and population migrates.

## lukesTake.primaryThesis

Geelong is where I would point a client who wants a real city, not a bet on a mining town or a coastal lifestyle story. The economic base is genuinely diverse across health, education, advanced manufacturing and defence, and the population growth is structural, not speculative. The catalysts here are not hypothetical; they are already underway and already funded.

---

## lukesTake.secondaryThesis

The part of this market I focus on is the established middle ring, not the new growth corridors. There is real supply pressure in the outer suburbs, and developers are still active. The inner and mid-ring, where land is genuinely constrained and tenant quality is strong, is where the medium-term hold case is most defensible regardless of what happens with interest rates in the short term.
